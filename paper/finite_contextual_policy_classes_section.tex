\section{Finite Contextual Policy Classes}
\label{sec:finite-contextual-policy}

The preceding sections formulate generator-augmented BAI for a finite set of arms. In many applications, however, the object to be selected is not a fixed action but a deployment rule that maps unit contexts to actions. Let \(W\sim\mathcal D\) denote the context of a sampled unit and let \(\mathcal A\) be an action space. Consider a finite class of deployment rules
\[
    \Pi=\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n\},\qquad \pi_i:\mathcal W\to\mathcal A.
\]
Pulling arm \(i\) means deploying rule \(\pi_i\) on a fresh unit with context \(W_{i,s}\), executing action \(\pi_i(W_{i,s})\), and observing
\[
    X_{i,s}=R(W_{i,s},\pi_i(W_{i,s})).
\]
A prediction model or LLM may also be queried on the same unit--policy description and return a proxy score
\[
    Y_{i,s}=G(W_{i,s},\pi_i,\pi_i(W_{i,s})).
\]
The target is the best rule in the finite class,
\[
    i^\star=\arg\max_i V(\pi_i),\qquad
    V(\pi_i)=\mathbb E_{W\sim\mathcal D}[R(W,\pi_i(W))].
\]
This is not unrestricted contextual bandit learning. The policy class is fixed in advance, and the goal is to identify the best rule under the target context distribution.

After marginalizing over \(W\), each \(\pi_i\) induces an ordinary paired observation distribution for \((X_{i,s},Y_{i,s})\), with reward mean \(p_i=V(\pi_i)\) and proxy mean \(m_i=\mathbb E[Y_{i,s}]\). The proxy mean may be biased and non-rank-preserving. The control-variate adjustment
\[
    Z_{i,s}(\beta)=X_{i,s}-\beta(Y_{i,s}-m_i)
\]
therefore preserves the policy value for every \(\beta\). Hence the algorithms and guarantees for paired-observation BAI apply directly to this finite-policy reduction, provided the marginal paired observations satisfy the assumptions of the main model.
